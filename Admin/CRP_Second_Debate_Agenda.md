## II. Key Changes Addressing Panel Feedback

### A. **DDL Justification (Dr. Wang's Feedback)**

**Original Concern:** Manuscript must justify AI-assisted learning as DDL and explain why poetry writing is a suitable research target.

**Changes Made:**
1. **Section 1 (Introduction)** now explicitly argues that AI-assisted poetry constitutes DDL by:
   - Redefining DDL as "data-driven decision making in language learning" rather than narrow corpus queries
   - Explaining LLMs as trained on massive corpora, functioning as "data-driven pattern providers"
   - Positioning AI outputs as "linguistic data to notice, analyze, and revise"
   
2. **Added explicit statement:** "AI can serve as a new DDL interface if learners treat its outputs as linguistic data to notice, analyze, and revise"

3. **Structural comparison:** Traditional DDL vs. AI-assisted DDL shown as "structurally similar in learning logic even if their interfaces differ"

**Discussion Points:**
- Does the redefinition of DDL adequately justify AI-mediated pattern presentation?
- Is the argument that AI functions as a "new DDL interface" convincing?

---

### B. **Poetry as Effable & Machine Logic (Dr. Checketts' Feedback)**

**Original Concern:** Poetry is not ineffable but effable; manuscript must justify why poetry writing needs or benefits from machine influence, especially given Heidegger's framing of poetry as opposite of machine logic.

**Changes Made:**
1. **Direct engagement with Heidegger (1984):** Acknowledged his position that poetry represents "the purest form of human artistic expression," fundamentally different from "machinery operation which based on scientific approaches, logic, and reasoning"

2. **Counter-argument via Strehovec (2023):** Introduced perspective that artistic creation "can be understood as a cognitive and goal-oriented activity" and poetry composition as "a certain type of reasoning and problem-solving"

3. **Position statement:** "Machine influence is pedagogically valid when it is bounded and transparent: AI can provide pattern data and alternative options, while human writers remain responsible for selection, rejection, and revision"

4. **Specific justification for novice writers:** "For beginners in writing, who often have ideas and affective goals but lack linguistic resources to realize them, AI-assisted poetry can still be DDL"

**Discussion Points:**
- Does the Heidegger-Strehovec dialectic adequately address the poetry-machine tension?
- Is the position on "bounded and transparent" machine influence defensible?
- Does the focus on novice writers constitute a limitation or appropriate scope?

---

### C. **Creativity Framework (Dr. Checketts & Dr. Hung's Feedback)**

**Original Concern:** Build a creativity framework in the discussion, potentially using Guilford's divergent and convergent creativity to avoid deficit framing of L2 learners.

**Changes Made:**
1. **Three-Interaction-Types Framework** developed as alternative to Guilford:
   - **Type A (Constraint Repair):** Normative pattern reinforcement → convergent creativity
   - **Type B (Exemplar Giving):** Model provision and selection → hybrid mode
   - **Type C (Surprise Harvest):** Serendipitous discovery → divergent creativity

2. **Theoretical integration:**
   - Coenen et al. (2022): serendipitous discovery in human-AI collaboration
   - Qian et al. (2023): cognitive load redistribution
   - Li (2025): strategic collaboration shifts
   - Boden (2004): divergent thinking and novelty in creative processes

3. **Parameter-scaffolding mechanism:** Framework explicitly links technical parameters to pedagogical scaffolding modes:
   - Low parameters (T=0.3, top-p=0.4) → Type A dominance → heavy scaffolding
   - High parameters (T=0.8, top-p=0.9) → Type C increase → light scaffolding

**Discussion Points:**
- Does the three-interaction-types framework adequately capture creative processes without Guilford's explicit divergent/convergent distinction?
- Is the parameter-interaction linkage theoretically sound?
- How does this framework avoid L2 deficit assumptions?

## III. Theoretical Contributions & Novel Claims

### Major Claims to Defend:

1. **Parameter manipulation = pedagogical lever:** "Parameter configuration functions as a pedagogical design lever for shifting between different DDL scaffolding modes"

2. **The Type B Paradox:** "Exemplar-giving interactions were consistently rated the most helpful, yet also the most likely to trigger feelings of reduced ownership"
   - Evidenced across all phases
   - Represents fundamental tension between efficiency and authorship

3. **Type C as co-creation mechanism:** "Creative agency and satisfaction emerged most strongly in Type C interactions through iterative reciprocity"
   - 7-fold increase in Type C under exploratory parameters
   - Case evidence: P09 (80% authorship), P12 (90% authorship)

4. **Learner-intuitive scaffolding:** "Parameters function as learner-intuitive pedagogical levers, consistently determining which interaction types become available"
   - Phase 2: 75% voluntarily selected exploratory
   - Phase 3: 66.7% chose exploratory, 62% switched modes

## IV. Methodological Strengths & Potential Concerns

### Strengths to Highlight:

1. **Three-phase design:** Progression from controlled experiment → naturalistic classroom → online environment strengthens ecological validity

2. **Platform design:** Custom-built platform (poetry.aitutor.ink) with prompt engineering to control AI behavior while manipulating parameters

3. **Multi-source data:** Chat histories, interaction coding, poem artifacts, reflection templates, panel discussions

4. **Convergent evidence:** Quantitative (chi-squared = 24.3, p < .001) and qualitative (participant narratives) alignment

### Potential Concerns to Address:

1. **Sample size:** N=30 (Phase 1: N=10, Phase 2: N=8, Phase 3: N=12)
   - Defense: Depth over breadth; rich qualitative data; three-phase triangulation

2. **L1 vs. L2 distinction:** Manuscript emphasizes "novice writers" over language background
   - Did this adequately address Dr. Hung's concern about L2 framing?
   - Future Study Plan mentions L1 control groups—should this be in main manuscript?

3. **Creativity operationalization:** Framework uses interaction types rather than established creativity measures
   - Strength: Context-specific, DDL-aligned
   - Concern: Lacks validated creativity scales

4. **Self-reported authorship:** Authorship percentages based on participant perception rather than computational attribution
   - Defense: Phenomenological validity matters for pedagogical experience
