# Panel Feedback and Revisions

## Panel Feedback (Key Points)

- Dr. Wang: AI tools have potential for data-driven learning (DDL), but the manuscript must justify AI-assisted learning as DDL and explain why poetry writing is a suitable research target despite its difference from academic writing.
- Dr. Checketts: Poetry is not ineffable but effable; Hiedinger's Holderland frames poetry writing as the opposite of machine logic. The manuscript should justify why poetry writing needs or benefits from machine influence.
- Dr. Checketts and Dr. Hung: Build a creativity framework in the discussion. Use Guilford's divergent and convergent creativity to avoid assuming L2 learners are deficient compared to L1 writers.
- Dr. Harrington: The research questions are too broad. Clarify a unified focus: AI tools in a university pedagogical context and their role in enhancing L2 learners' authorship/ownership, creative agency, and satisfaction in poetry writing. Also explore Oulipo as a relevant creative constraint tradition.
- Dr. Hung: Clarify why English poems and reaffirm the pedagogical university context rather than off-campus or non-pedagogical use.

## Future Study Plan (Methodological Changes)

- Design: Use a mixed-methods, quasi-experimental design with L2 and L1 groups in parallel, both using AI tools in the same pedagogical tasks.
- Sampling: Define proficiency bands and ensure comparable exposure to poetry instruction; document participants' prior AI experience.
- Tasks: Standardize a sequence of poetry writing tasks with staged AI use (no-AI draft, AI-supported revision, reflection).
- Data sources: Collect drafts, AI prompts/outputs, revision logs, reflective journals, and post-task interviews.
- Measures: Operationalize authorship/ownership, creative agency, satisfaction, and divergent/convergent creativity with rubrics and validated scales where possible.
- Analysis: Use thematic analysis for reflections/interviews and quantitative comparisons for rubric and scale scores; triangulate across data sources.
- Controls: Add an L1 control group to avoid deficit framing and include a no-AI condition if feasible to isolate AI impact.
- Ethics and transparency: Document AI tool versions, prompts, and usage policies; include a disclosure protocol for student AI use.
- Deliverables: Pre-register the study design, publish a task protocol, and provide an anonymized dataset map.
