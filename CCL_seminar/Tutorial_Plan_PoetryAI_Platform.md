# Tutorial Plan: Using Poetry AI Platform for L2 Creative Writing
## CCL Seminar Phase 2 - Participant Learning Experience

**Prepared for**: Hong Kong Baptist University CCL Seminar — Session 2  
**Platform**: poetry.aitutor.ink  
**Based on**: Phase 1 Research Data (N=10 participants, 500+ interactions)  
**Target Audience**: L2 English learners interested in AI-assisted creative writing  
**Duration**: 120 minutes (includes 5 phases: 4 core tutorial + 1 Phase 1 data sharing)  
**Room Setup**: 2 experimental conditions (Room A: Structured | Room C: Exploratory)

---

## PART 1: TUTORIAL OVERVIEW

### Learning Objectives
By the end of this tutorial, participants will:
1. Understand how AI parameter settings affect creative writing support
2. Navigate the Poetry AI platform interface and core features
3. Experience different "interaction types" (Constraint Repair, Exemplar Giving, Surprise Harvest)
4. Reflect on authorship perception and creative agency in AI-assisted poetry
5. Develop literacy in "parameter awareness" for adaptive AI usage

### Evidence Foundation
This tutorial is grounded in Phase 1 empirical findings:
- **7× difference in creative suggestions**: Exploratory parameters (temp 0.8, top-p 0.9) generated 35% "Surprise Harvest" interactions vs. 5% in structured conditions
- **100% poem completion rate**: All Phase 1 participants successfully created at least one complete poem
- **20-40 message exchanges per participant**: Typical conversation depth sufficient for meaningful creative work
- **Key finding**: 75% of participants valued "helpful examples," but examples correlated with *reduced* authorship perception (r = -.58)—exposing the need for parameter-adjusted scaffolding

## PART 2: SESSION STRUCTURE (90 minutes)

### **PHASE 1: PLATFORM ORIENTATION (10 minutes)**

**Objective**: Build technical comfort and establish baseline understanding

#### 1.1 Login & Interface Navigation (5 minutes)
**What participants do:**
- Log in with assigned credentials
- Familiarize themselves with the chatbot interface
- Explore navigation buttons: Poetry Forms selector, Chat history, Reflection template
- Test sending a simple message ("Hello, I'm ready to write poetry")

**Facilitator talking points:**
- "This platform is designed to feel like you're writing *with* an AI partner, not being judged by one"
- "Everything you write is captured automatically—you can see your full conversation history at any time"
- "You control the pace. The AI responds quickly, but you decide when to continue"

**Success indicator**: Participants can send a message and receive a response

#### 1.2 Room Assignment & Parameter Introduction (5 minutes)
**What happens:**
- Assign participants to two room conditions:
  - **Room A (Structured)**: Temperature 0.3, Top-p 0.4 — AI emphasizes form accuracy and grammatical correctness
  - **Room C (Exploratory)**: Temperature 0.8, Top-p 0.9 — AI emphasizes unexpected ideas and creative divergence
- **NO disclosure of parameter differences** (participants don't know rooms differ)
- Participants simply receive a "room assignment" without knowing parameters affect their AI experience

**Facilitator talking points:**
- "You've been randomly assigned to a room. Each room's AI has slightly different 'personality.' We want to see how you naturally adapt."
- "Don't overthink it—just write naturally and let us know what you notice."

---

### **PHASE 2: AI-ASSISTED POETRY WRITING (40 minutes)**

**Objective**: Generate authentic creative work while experiencing parameter effects

#### 2.1 Poetry Form Selection (2 minutes)
**What participants choose:**
- Available forms: Haiku, Couplet, Quatrain, Free Verse, Limerick, Acrostic
- Recommendation: Start with Haiku (simplest structure, quickest to complete)

**Facilitator guidance:**
- "Choose a form you're familiar with, or one you want to learn"
- "The AI will help you understand the requirements. No prior knowledge needed."

#### 2.2 Creative Writing Conversation (35 minutes)
**What participants experience:**

**Structured Parameter Condition (Rooms A & B):**
- AI provides **Type A (Constraint Repair)**: "That's 8 syllables. Haiku needs exactly 5. Try: [concrete suggestion]"
- AI provides **Type B (Exemplar Giving)**: "Here are three opening lines that capture autumn:" + 3 options
- AI emphasizes form accuracy, grammar, conventional patterns
- **Expected outcome**: Technically correct poems, participants feel supported but less creative agency
- **Sample exchange**: (From Phase 1 research)
  - USER: "In the factory of dreams, where wonder grows"
  - AI (Structured): "That's a beautiful opening! For your next line, let's continue building this dreamlike world. Here are a few options: (1) Rabbits scurry with visions in tow (2) Machines hum softly, stitching joy and woe"

**Exploratory Parameter Condition (Rooms C & D):**
- AI provides **Type C (Surprise Harvest)**: "What if 'factory' was actually 'library'? Or 'hospital'? How would that shift your imagery?"
- AI suggests unexpected metaphors, structural experimentation, thematic pivots
- AI emphasizes novelty, divergence, personal voice
- **Expected outcome**: More creative/experimental poems, participants feel more authorship but less technical support
- **Sample exchange**: (Contrasting with structured above)
  - USER: "In the factory of dreams, where wonder grows"
  - AI (Exploratory): "Factory—interesting choice! Most people think 'garden' or 'forest' for dreams. A factory suggests mechanical repetition. Are you exploring that contrast intentionally? Could work beautifully with surreal/dystopian themes. Where do you want to take it?"

**Participant instructions:**
1. Start with a single line, theme, or image that interests you
2. Have a conversation with the AI—don't wait for perfection
3. Try at least 20 message exchanges (most Phase 1 participants did 20-40)
4. Complete at least one full poem in your chosen form
5. If you finish early, start a second poem or try a different form

**Research-based recommendations for facilitators:**
- Circulate and observe (don't interrupt the creative flow)
- If participants seem stuck: "Share your unfinished thought with the AI—it's often more helpful than waiting for perfection"
- If participants seem frustrated with form constraints: "Ask the AI to explain the form differently, or switch to free verse"
- **Monitor parameter effects invisibly**: Notice which rooms produce more technical vs. exploratory responses (you'll see dramatic differences)

**Success indicators:**
- Participant completes at least one poem
- Participant engages in 15+ exchanges with AI
- Visible evidence of revision or iteration (crossing out, rewording)

### **PHASE 3: IMMEDIATE REFLECTION (15 minutes)**

**Objective**: Capture participants' lived experience while memories are vivid

#### 3.1 Structured Reflection Template (10 minutes)
**What participants complete:**

**Section A: Authorship & Agency**
- (1-5 scale) "I felt like the creator of this poem" 
- (1-5 scale) "The AI understood what I wanted to express"
- Open-ended: "Describe a moment when you felt most creative / most constrained"

**Section B: AI Interaction Experience**
- (Multiple choice) "The AI's suggestions were most helpful when they:"
  - [ ] Pointed out grammar or form errors
  - [ ] Gave me specific word or line options
  - [ ] Suggested unexpected directions I hadn't considered
  - [ ] Other: ______

- (1-5 scale) "How natural did the conversation with the AI feel?"
- (1-5 scale) "How helpful were the AI's suggestions overall?"

**Section C: Creative Satisfaction**
- (1-5 scale) "I'm satisfied with my final poem"
- (1-5 scale) "This process increased my confidence in poetry writing"
- Open-ended: "What would make this experience better?"

**Section D: Parameter Awareness (Only if revealing conditions)**
- If you're running the aware condition: "Did you notice the AI's 'personality' or style? Describe it."
- (1-5 scale) "How much did parameter differences affect your experience?"

**Phase 1 research context to share with participants (optional):**
*"In our previous study, we found that different AI settings create very different creative experiences. Some rooms emphasized getting the form exactly right. Others encouraged experimental directions. Neither is 'better'—they support different kinds of learning. We want to understand which works best for you."*

#### 3.2 Artifact Collection (5 minutes)
- Automatically capture final poems from chat history
- Optional: Photograph/scan handwritten notes, revision marks, brainstorms
- Create a simple "Poem Gallery" document for Phase 2 seminar

---

### **PHASE 4: PANEL DISCUSSION & MEANING-MAKING (15 minutes)**

**Objective**: Generate collective insights about parameter effects and AI-assisted creativity

#### 4.1 Room-by-Room Sharing (5 minutes)
**Structure**: Each room (A & C) discusses together for 2.5 minutes

**Facilitator prompts:**
- "What did you notice about how the AI responded to your writing?"
- "Did the AI feel more like a teacher, a coach, or a collaborator?"
- "What surprised you about the experience?"
- "What frustrated you?"

**Facilitator's hidden observation task:**
- Listen for mentions of "grammar," "correct," "errors" → likely Structured room
- Listen for mentions of "surprised," "unexpected," "never thought of that" → likely Exploratory room
- These natural language cues reveal parameter effects without announcing them

#### 4.2 Cross-Room Comparative Discussion (10 minutes)
**Facilitator reveals parameter differences:**

*"Thank you for those reflections. Here's something we want to share: You were actually using AI with different settings. Room A had AI configured to prioritize form accuracy and provide specific options—we call this 'Structured.' Room C had AI configured to generate unexpected ideas and explore different directions—we call this 'Exploratory.' Neither is better. They're designed for different creative goals."*

**Follow-up discussion prompts:**
- "Looking back at your conversation, can you identify moments of Type A (form correction), Type B (example options), or Type C (unexpected suggestions)?"
- "Which interaction type was most helpful? Why?"
- "When might you want the 'structured' support? When might you want the 'exploratory' support?"
- "How did the different room conditions affect your sense of authorship—did you feel like you were creating or selecting?"
- "What does this tell us about AI's role in learning to write?"

### **PHASE 5: PHASE 1 DATA SHARING & COLLABORATIVE INTERPRETATION (30 minutes)**

**Objective**: Show participants the research evidence that explains what they just experienced, invite them to compare their experience with Phase 1 findings

#### 5.1 Parameter Effects Visualization (8 minutes)
**What you present:**

**Interaction Type Distribution Comparison:**
```
Phase 1 Research Results (N=10 participants, ~500 message exchanges)

                    STRUCTURED (Room A)      EXPLORATORY (Room C)
Type A (Repair)           60%                      20%
Type B (Exemplar)         35%                      45%
Type C (Surprise)          5%                      35%  ← 7× DIFFERENCE
```

**Visual recommendation**: Create a simple bar chart or stacked bar graph showing this distribution

**Facilitator talking points:**
*"This is what we found in our Phase 1 study. When we coded every AI response into three types, we saw dramatic differences between rooms. The most striking finding: exploratory parameters generated 7 times more 'surprise harvest' moments—unexpected directions you wouldn't have discovered on your own."*

**Invite participant comparison:**
- "Looking at your own chat logs, which room's distribution matches what you experienced?"
- "Can you count your Type A, B, and C moments? Roughly what percentage?"
- "Did you notice the difference in real time, or only looking back?"

#### 5.2 The Authorship Perception Paradox (8 minutes)
**What you present:**

**Phase 1 Finding:**
```
Self-Reported Measures (1-5 scale):

"I felt like the creator of this poem"
  - Structured Room (A): 3.2/5
  - Exploratory Room (C): 4.1/5 
  - Difference: Exploratory room felt MORE like creators

"The AI's suggestions were helpful"
  - Structured Room (A): 4.6/5 ← MORE helpful
  - Exploratory Room (C): 3.9/5
  - Pattern: MORE help ≠ MORE authorship
```

**Key insight to unpack:**
*"Here's the interesting paradox we discovered: In the structured room, people found the AI much more helpful—it gave them specific options and corrections. But they felt LESS like they were creating the poem. In the exploratory room, people felt MORE like creators, even though they got fewer direct suggestions. This is the creative paradox: help can undermine ownership."*

**Interactive reflection:**
- "Does this match your experience? How much did you feel like the creator?"
- "The helpful suggestions—did they feel like support or like the AI was taking over?"
- "What kind of help would increase BOTH authorship AND usefulness?"

**Deeper conversation starter:**
*"This finding changes how we think about 'good teaching.' It's not just about providing help. It's about the RIGHT KIND of help for the learner's goals. For creative writing, sometimes asking questions (like the exploratory AI) helps you feel more ownership than giving answers (like the structured AI), even if the answers are good."*

#### 5.3 Satisfaction & Completion Data (5 minutes)
**What you present:**

```
Phase 1 Outcomes:
- Poem Completion Rate: 100% (all 10 participants completed ≥1 poem)
- Satisfaction with Final Poem: 75% rated 4-5/5
- Avg Conversation Length: 20-40 exchanges per participant
- Willingness to Use Again: 90% said "yes"
```

**Key point:**
*"One finding surprised us: participants in BOTH rooms were equally satisfied with their final poems and equally willing to use the platform again. The parameter differences didn't affect overall satisfaction—but they affected HOW they experienced the process. For you today, we're collecting the same data. We'll compare and see if the same patterns appear in a new group."*

#### 5.4 What This Means for Educators & Learners (9 minutes)
**Facilitator-led discussion:**

**For educators:**
*"This research suggests that 'parameter literacy' is becoming an important teaching skill. Just like we learn to choose textbooks, lesson structures, and feedback styles for different learners, we may need to learn to configure AI tools for different learning goals. Do you want students to master form accuracy? Use structured parameters. Do you want them to explore creative boundaries? Use exploratory parameters."*

**For learners:**
*"And for you as learners—understanding parameters helps you advocate for what you need. If you feel constrained by too many 'helpful' suggestions, you can ask the AI to be more exploratory. If you feel lost without structure, you can ask for more guidance. Parameter awareness is a learner skill."*

**Discussion prompts:**
1. "How might you use parameter adjustment in your own writing process?"
2. "When would structured support be more helpful? When would exploratory support work better?"
3. "What does it mean to learn 'with' AI versus learning 'from' AI?"
4. "If you became a teacher, how would you use these insights?"
5. "What other AI tools have you used? Did they feel more structured or exploratory? How did that affect your experience?"

**Optional deeper question** (if time/group energy allows):
*"Our Phase 1 data shows that Type C 'surprise harvest' moments help people feel more creative ownership. But many people also worry that AI might be 'too random' or unhelpful. How do we balance being surprised by AI's suggestions with trusting that it's actually supporting our learning?"*

## PART 3: FACILITATOR GUIDANCE & RESEARCH INTEGRATION

### **Managing Parameter Differences Transparently**

**Phase 1 Model (RECOMMENDED for research purposes):**
- Don't disclose parameter differences until Phase 4
- Assign rooms randomly to capture natural parameter effects
- Analyze interaction types during Phase 2 while facilitating
- Reveal differences strategically in Phase 4 for metacognitive reflection

**Alternative: Full Transparency Model (for learning purposes)**
- Disclose parameter differences at beginning of Phase 2
- Invite participants to intentionally compare modes
- Have them switch conditions mid-session to directly experience effects
- More educational but less "pure" research data

### **Handling the "Helpful but Alienating" Paradox**

**Research finding**: Type B (Exemplar Giving) correlates with reduced authorship (r = -.58)

**What this means**: Participants found the AI's suggestions genuinely helpful for completing poems, but those same suggestions sometimes undermined their sense of creative authorship.

**How to facilitate this insight:**
1. **During reflection** (Phase 3): Ask, "Did the AI's suggestions feel helpful? How did they affect your sense of being the author?"
2. **During discussion** (Phase 4): Share Phase 1 finding and invite participants to interpret: "Why might helpful suggestions sometimes undermine creative ownership?"
3. **Deeper conversation**: "What kind of help *doesn't* undermine authorship? How could an AI partner support you without taking over?"

**Possible participant insights they often generate:**
- "When the AI gave options, I felt like I was just picking, not creating"
- "When the AI asked questions, I felt like I was discovering my own ideas"
- "I needed the structure for the form, but I wanted freedom in the content"
- "It depends—sometimes I want quick answers, sometimes I want to struggle productively"

---

### **Monitoring Engagement & Completion**

**Success benchmarks from Phase 1:**
- **Target completion rate**: 100% (all participants created ≥1 poem)
- **Target interaction depth**: 20-40 message exchanges (range: 20-60)
- **Target reflection form completion**: 100% (critical for research data)
- **Target panel participation**: 100%

**If participants are struggling:**

| Issue | Solution |
|-------|----------|
| "I don't know what to write about" | Suggest AI prompts: "Ask the AI for a poetry prompt" or provide starter themes (loneliness, joy, memory, growth) |
| "My poem doesn't feel finished" | Explain rough drafts are valid; poem evolution is the goal, not perfection |
| "The AI keeps giving wrong suggestions" | Check: Is this parameter-appropriate? Structured room should give form-focused help. If misaligned, facilitator can provide direct guidance |
| "I finished too fast" | Encourage second poem in different form, or deeper revision of first poem |

## PART 5: SAMPLE INTERACTION TRANSCRIPTS
### **Examples of Three Interaction Types** (from Phase 1 research)

#### **TYPE A: Constraint Repair** (Structured Rooms)
```
PARTICIPANT: In the factory of dreams where wonder grows

AI: That's a beautiful opening line! But let's check the syllable count:
"In-the-fac-tor-y-of-dreams-where-won-der-grows" = 12 syllables

For a haiku opening, we need exactly 5. Here are three options:
1. "A factory of dreams" (5 syllables)
2. "In dreams, factories grow" (6 syllables - close, could work with revision)
3. "Wonder grows in dreams" (5 syllables)

Which direction appeals to you? Or would you like me to suggest others?

PARTICIPANT: Maybe "Wonder grows in dreams"

AI: Perfect! That's exactly 5 syllables and captures your theme beautifully. 
Now for your middle line, we need 7 syllables. What feeling or image would
you like to explore next?
```
**Analysis**: Type A provides corrective guidance ("that's 12 syllables, haiku needs 5"), then offers specific alternatives. Participant feels supported but also told what to do.

---

#### **TYPE B: Exemplar Giving** (Structured Rooms)
```
PARTICIPANT: I want to write about loneliness

AI: Loneliness is a profound theme. Here are three different approaches:

**Approach 1 - Physical isolation**:
"Empty subway train at midnight" (suggests solitude in crowded places)

**Approach 2 - Emotional absence**:
"Your silence fills my room" (suggests loneliness with someone present)

**Approach 3 - Temporal**:
"The hours stretch endlessly" (suggests time becomes weight when alone)

Which resonates most with what you want to express?

PARTICIPANT: I like approach 1... but different

AI: Great! Let's modify it. What details would make "empty subway" feel more
personal or unexpected? An emotion? A time? A sensory detail?
```
**Analysis**: Type B provides concrete examples as scaffolds. Participant chooses and adapts rather than generating from scratch. Helpful, but shifts agency toward selection.

---

#### **TYPE C: Surprise Harvest** (Exploratory Rooms)
```
PARTICIPANT: I want to write about loneliness

AI: Loneliness—interesting. Most people write about empty spaces or missing people.
But what about the *opposite*? What if you're lonely *in a crowd*? Or what if
loneliness is actually a *gift*—the freedom to think your own thoughts?

What happens if you explore loneliness as:
- A state of clarity rather than pain?
- A crowded space (party, subway) where you're still alone?
- An animal or object feeling lonely (not a person)?

Or here's a wild idea: What if nothing is lonely? What if everything is connected
in ways we can't see? Where does *that* tension take you?

PARTICIPANT: Hmm... a crowded place... but it feels safe somehow?

AI: OHHH—that's an interesting contradiction. Safety *in* solitude within crowds.
That's almost paradoxical. Could explore: subway rides, libraries (quiet but
populated), or spaces like airports where everyone is alone together. What image
comes to mind?
```
**Analysis**: Type C offers unexpected directions ("What if loneliness is a gift?"), inverses ("lonely in a crowd"), and divergent frameworks. Participant generates novel possibilities rather than selecting from options.

## PART 7: FACILITATOR SCRIPT & KEY TALKING POINTS

### **Opening (Phase 1)**
*"Welcome! Today you're going to experience something most people haven't done yet: writing poetry *with* an AI partner. This isn't about AI replacing you as a writer. It's about understanding how to work collaboratively with AI—how to let it support your creativity without taking it over. You've been randomly assigned to Room A or Room C. Each room's AI will feel slightly different, though you won't know exactly how. Just write naturally and notice what you experience. We'll talk about what you noticed afterward, and I'll show you some surprising research findings. Your poems are completely yours, and so is the creative process. Let's begin."*

### **During Phase 2 (If needed)**
*"You're doing great. Just keep writing—the conversational flow is more important than perfection. If you get stuck, tell the AI you're stuck. That's actually really valuable data for us. And remember: there's no 'right poem.' Whatever you create reflects your choices and your voice."*

### **Revealing Parameters (Phase 4)**
*"Thank you for those reflections. I want to share something interesting. You've actually been using AI with different settings. Room A had AI configured to prioritize form accuracy and provide specific options—we call this 'Structured.' Room C had AI configured to generate unexpected ideas and explore different directions—we call this 'Exploratory.'"*

*"Does that match what you experienced? Look back at your chats—can you see Type A moments where the AI corrected you, Type B moments where it gave options, Type C moments where it surprised you?"*

### **Transition to Phase 5**
*"Before we wrap up, I want to show you something really interesting. You've just experienced what we studied in our Phase 1 research. And the data we collected shows some surprising patterns. Let's look at what we found, and you can see if your experience matches what others discovered."*

### **Closing (after Phase 5)**
*"What you've experienced today—and the research findings you just saw—show us that 'parameter literacy' is becoming an important skill. Understanding how to configure AI tools for different learning goals, recognizing when you need structure versus exploration, and feeling empowered to ask the AI for what you need—that's the future. As AI becomes more common in schools and workplaces, this awareness will be genuinely valuable. Thank you for not just participating in this tutorial, but for contributing real data that helps us understand how people learn with AI. Your poems and reflections are part of the research."*
