# Abstract

Does generative AI represent the death of Data-Driven Learning, or its evolution? This study addresses Crosthwaite's (2023) fundamental challenge by investigating how AI parameter configuration shapes learning outcomes in L2 creative writing. Through a 2×2 experimental design (N=10, target N=20), we manipulated temperature and top-p settings (structured: 0.3/0.4 vs. exploratory: 0.8/0.9) and parameter awareness (aware vs. unaware) while Hong Kong university students composed poetry with AI assistance. Findings reveal that parameter configuration functions as a pedagogical lever producing dramatically different interaction patterns: exploratory parameters generated 7× more Type C "surprise harvest" interactions than structured parameters (35% vs. 5%, p<.001), directly impacting authorship perception and creative satisfaction. Crucially, 75% of participants rated Type B "exemplar giving" as most helpful, yet Type B frequency negatively correlated with authorship perception (r=-.58), exposing the "helpful but alienating" paradox of over-scaffolding. Our findings demonstrate that AI neither kills nor simply continues DDL—rather, parameter configuration determines whether AI functions as pattern-enforcer undermining creative agency or pattern-extender enabling corpus-divergent discovery. This research establishes parameter literacy as essential professional competence for educators, suggesting that DDL's future hinges not on AI's capabilities but on our capacity to configure it as adaptive pedagogical scaffold. When educators gain control over temperature and top-p settings, AI transforms from potential threat into powerful partner advancing DDL's evolution in the generative era.

**Keywords:** Data-Driven Learning; generative AI; parameter configuration; adaptive scaffolding; L2 creative writing
