Current commercial AI systems treat parameters as hidden implementation details, leaving educators unable to deliberately calibrate scaffolding intensity in L2 creative writing. This research investigates how Large Language Model parameter configuration functions as a pedagogical design lever for adaptive scaffolding. We designed a purpose-built platform (poetry.aitutor.ink) decoupling parameters from prompt design, enabling direct measurement of how temperature and top-p settings shape learner interaction patterns, creative agency, and authorship perception in AI-assisted poetry writing. Through three-phase mixed-methods study (N=30), we examined structured (T=0.25, P=0.35) versus exploratory (T=0.75, P=0.85) parameter conditions. Findings reveal exploratory parameters generated creative divergence interactions at 7× the rate of structured parameters (35% vs. 5%), producing 6× higher authorship perception (62.5% vs. 10%) and doubled creative satisfaction. Analysis uncovered the "helpful but alienating" paradox: learners rated heavily scaffolded interactions most helpful, yet these negatively correlated with authorship claims (r=-.58), demonstrating that pedagogical utility diverges from creative agency. Critically, learner autonomy emerged as meta-variable—identical scaffolding transformed from frustrating when imposed to pragmatically accepted when voluntarily chosen. These findings establish parameter configuration as field-worthy research domain, demonstrating that AI scaffolding effectiveness depends on deliberate configuration choices currently invisible to educators, enabling design of responsive systems adapting scaffolding to learner needs.
