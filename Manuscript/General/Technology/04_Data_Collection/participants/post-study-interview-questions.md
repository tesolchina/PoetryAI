# Post-Study Interview Questions for PoetryAI Participants

**Study:** L2 Poetry Writing with AI Parameter Effects  
**Researcher:** Yu Ruobin, HKBU Department of English  
**Interview Duration:** 30-45 minutes per participant  
**Format:** Semi-structured, online (video call)  
**Date:** November 22, 2025

---

## Interview Protocol Overview

**Purpose:** To explore participants' experiences with AI-assisted poetry writing, particularly focusing on:
1. How they used the AI chatbot (inquiry moves and strategies)
2. What support they received and how they used it (scaffolding)
3. How they experienced collaboration with AI (collaborative dynamics)
4. Which interaction patterns they found most useful (interaction type preferences)

**Structure:** 
- Warm-up questions (5 mins)
- RQ-specific questions (25-30 mins)
- Closing reflections (5-10 mins)

---

## WARM-UP QUESTIONS (5 minutes)

### General Experience
1. **Overall impression:** Can you tell me about your overall experience using the PoetryAI chatbot today?
   - *Probe:* What stood out to you most?
   - *Probe:* Was there anything surprising or unexpected?

2. **Comfort level:** How comfortable did you feel interacting with the AI chatbot?
   - *Probe:* Did your comfort level change as the session progressed?
   - *Probe:* Were there moments when you felt more or less confident?

3. **Prior experience:** Have you used AI writing tools before (like ChatGPT, Grammarly, etc.)?
   - *If yes:* How was this experience similar or different?
   - *If no:* What were your expectations going in?

---

## RQ1: PARAMETER EFFECTS ON INQUIRY MOVES (10 minutes)

**Research Question:** How do parameter settings condition learners' inquiry moves and appeals to exemplars during poem co-creation?

### A. Creativity Strategies

4. **Idea generation:** When you were writing your poem, how did you come up with ideas?
   - *Probe:* Did you bring your own ideas first, or did you ask the chatbot for suggestions?
   - *Probe:* Can you describe a specific moment when you generated a creative idea? Where did it come from?

5. **Chatbot suggestions:** How did you use the suggestions the chatbot gave you?
   - *Probe:* Did you accept them as-is, modify them, or reject them?
   - *Probe:* Can you give me an example of when you modified a suggestion? What made you change it?

6. **Creative exploration:** Did you feel like you were exploring different creative directions, or focusing on one clear path?
   - *Probe:* What influenced this approach?
   - *Probe:* Did the chatbot's responses encourage more exploration or more focus?

### B. Stylistic Patterning

7. **Style awareness:** Did you think about the style or "voice" of your poem while writing?
   - *Probe:* How did the chatbot influence the style of your poem?
   - *Probe:* Did you notice any patterns in how the chatbot responded to you?

8. **Language choices:** When choosing words or phrases, what guided your decisions?
   - *Probe:* Did the chatbot help you think about word choices differently?
   - *Probe:* Can you give an example of when you made a deliberate stylistic choice?

### C. Appeals to Exemplars

9. **Examples and models:** Did you ask the chatbot to show you examples of poems or lines?
   - *If yes:* How did you use those examples? Did you copy, adapt, or use them for inspiration?
   - *If no:* Why didn't you ask for examples?

10. **Learning from patterns:** Did you notice any patterns or structures in what the chatbot suggested?
    - *Probe:* Did these patterns help you understand how to write poetry better?
    - *Probe:* Can you point to a specific pattern you learned?

### D. Parameter Awareness (if applicable)

**For AWARE participants only:**

11. **Parameter understanding:** You could see the temperature and top-p settings. Did you understand what these meant?
    - *Probe:* How did you think these settings affected the chatbot's responses?
    - *Probe:* Did knowing about these settings change how you interacted with the chatbot?

12. **Parameter influence:** Did you wish you could adjust these settings? Why or why not?
    - *Probe:* If you could change them, what would you change and why?

---

## RQ2: SCAFFOLDING PATTERNS (8 minutes)

**Research Question:** What forms of teacher/peer scaffolding emerge, and how do learners take up this scaffolding across parameter conditions?

### A. AI Scaffolding

13. **Chatbot support:** In what ways did the chatbot help you or support your writing?
    - *Probe:* Can you describe a moment when the chatbot's help was particularly useful?
    - *Probe:* Were there times when the chatbot's help was too much or not enough?

14. **Types of help:** What kind of help did you get from the chatbot?
    - *Prompt if needed:* Did it help with ideas, structure, word choice, grammar, something else?
    - *Probe:* Which type of help was most valuable to you?

15. **Taking up scaffolding:** When the chatbot made suggestions, how did you decide whether to use them?
    - *Probe:* Can you walk me through a specific example?
    - *Probe:* Did you feel like you were learning from the chatbot, or just following instructions?

### B. Peer Scaffolding (if applicable)

16. **Group discussion experience:** During the in-group discussion, what did you talk about with your peers?
    - *Probe:* Did they give you ideas or feedback that changed your thinking?
    - *Probe:* How was peer feedback different from chatbot feedback?

17. **Collaborative learning:** Did discussing with peers help you understand AI-assisted poetry writing better?
    - *Probe:* Can you give a specific example?
    - *Probe:* Would you have preferred more or less peer interaction?

### C. Self-Scaffolding

18. **Independent strategies:** Were there moments when you figured things out on your own, without help from the chatbot?
    - *Probe:* Can you describe what happened?
    - *Probe:* How did those moments feel compared to when you relied on the chatbot?

---

## RQ3: COLLABORATIVE DYNAMICS (8 minutes)

**Research Question:** How do collaborative dynamics (evaluation, negotiation, co-creation) unfold and vary by parameter settings?

### A. Evaluation

19. **Quality judgment:** How did you decide whether a line or suggestion was "good"?
    - *Probe:* Did you judge your own ideas differently from the chatbot's suggestions?
    - *Probe:* What criteria did you use? (emotional impact, imagery, grammar, etc.)

20. **Chatbot evaluation:** Did the chatbot ever evaluate or comment on your ideas?
    - *Probe:* How did you feel about that feedback?
    - *Probe:* Did you agree or disagree with the chatbot's evaluations?

### B. Negotiation of Meaning

21. **Clarifying intentions:** Were there times when you had to explain to the chatbot what you meant or wanted?
    - *Probe:* Can you describe what happened?
    - *Probe:* Did the chatbot understand your intention?

22. **Disagreement:** Did you ever disagree with the chatbot's suggestions?
    - *If yes:* What did you disagree about? How did you handle it?
    - *If no:* Why do you think you agreed with everything?

23. **Metalinguistic discussion:** Did you talk about language itself with the chatbot? (Like discussing why a word works, how to structure a line, etc.)
    - *Probe:* Can you give an example?
    - *Probe:* Did these discussions help your understanding?

### C. Co-Construction

24. **Creative partnership:** Did you feel like you and the chatbot were creating the poem together, or were you creating it alone with the chatbot as a tool?
    - *Probe:* What made you feel this way?
    - *Probe:* Can you point to a specific line or section that was truly co-created?

25. **Authorship:** Who do you think is the author of your poem?
    - *Probe:* How much of it feels like "yours" vs. the chatbot's?
    - *Probe:* Does it matter to you?

26. **Control and agency:** Did you feel in control of the creative process?
    - *Probe:* Were there moments when you felt the chatbot took over?
    - *Probe:* Were there moments when you felt the chatbot wasn't contributing enough?

## RQ4: INTERACTION TYPE RANKING (8 minutes)

**Research Question:** Which interaction types are most useful for instruction based on frequency, sequencing, and impact on revision and metalinguistic talk?

### Present Interaction Type Examples

**[Show participant three interaction type examples from their own chat history]:**

**Type A - Constraint Repair (Diagnostic/Corrective):**
- Example: When the chatbot identifies a technical issue (syllable count, grammar, structure) and helps you fix it
- Pattern: Diagnosis → Repair suggestion → You implement fix

**Type B - Content Enhancement (Exemplar-based):**
- Example: When you're stuck and the chatbot provides example lines or phrases you can choose from
- Pattern: You show need → Chatbot provides options → You select or adapt

**Type C - Surprise Harvest (Exploratory):**
- Example: When the chatbot offers an unexpected idea or direction you hadn't thought of
- Pattern: Open exploration → Unexpected suggestion → You evaluate and possibly pursue

### Ranking Questions

27. **Type identification:** Looking at your conversation, which type of interaction happened most often?
    - *Probe:* Why do you think that was?
    - *Probe:* Did you notice yourself seeking out certain types of interactions?

28. **Most helpful type:** Which type of interaction was most helpful for your writing today?
    - *Probe:* Why was it helpful?
    - *Probe:* Can you give a specific example?

29. **Learning impact:** Which type helped you learn the most about poetry writing?
    - *Probe:* What did you learn?
    - *Probe:* Is "most helpful" different from "most learning"? How?

30. **Preference ranking:** If you could design your ideal AI poetry assistant, what proportion of each type would you want?
    - *Probe:* Would you want mostly Type A (correction), Type B (examples), Type C (surprises), or a mix?
    - *Probe:* Would this change depending on your skill level?

31. **Sequencing:** Did the order of these interaction types matter?
    - *Probe:* For example, did you need Type B (examples) before you could handle Type C (surprises)?
    - *Probe:* Were certain types more useful at the beginning vs. end of your writing?

32. **Revision impact:** Which type of interaction led you to revise your poem the most?
    - *Probe:* Why do you think that type triggered more revision?
    - *Probe:* Were those revisions improvements?

---

## CLOSING REFLECTIONS (5-10 minutes)

### Overall Usefulness

33. **Instructional value:** If you were learning to write poetry in a class, would you want to use this AI chatbot?
    - *Probe:* Why or why not?
    - *Probe:* How would you want it to be used in class?

34. **Learning outcomes:** What did you learn today about poetry writing?
    - *Probe:* Would you have learned this without the AI?
    - *Probe:* What did you learn about working with AI?

35. **Comparison to traditional instruction:** How does this compare to learning poetry from a teacher or textbook?
    - *Probe:* What are the advantages and disadvantages?
    - *Probe:* What would be the ideal combination?

### Improvements

36. **What worked well:** What did the chatbot do really well?
    - *Probe:* What should definitely stay the same?

37. **What needs improvement:** If you could change one thing about the chatbot, what would it be?
    - *Probe:* How would that change improve your experience?

38. **Missing features:** Was there anything you wanted the chatbot to do that it couldn't?
    - *Probe:* What would that feature look like?

### Final Thoughts

39. **Creative expression:** Did using the AI help or hinder your creative expression?
    - *Probe:* In what ways?
    - *Probe:* Did you feel like you could still be creative, or did the AI limit you?

40. **Future use:** Would you use this tool again for poetry writing or other creative writing?
    - *Probe:* In what situations would you use it?
    - *Probe:* In what situations would you avoid it?

41. **Advice for others:** What advice would you give to another student about to use this AI poetry assistant?
    - *Probe:* What should they know going in?
    - *Probe:* What strategies worked best for you?

42. **Any other thoughts:** Is there anything else about your experience that we haven't talked about?
    - *Probe:* Anything that surprised you?
    - *Probe:* Anything you want to emphasize?

## INTERVIEWER NOTES

### Probing Strategies
- Always ask for **specific examples** from their session
- Use **"Can you tell me more about that?"** to encourage elaboration
- Ask **"Why?"** and **"How?"** to get deeper understanding
- **Mirror language** participants use to build rapport
- **Pause and wait** - give participants time to think
- **Follow unexpected threads** - if something interesting emerges, explore it

### Observation Notes During Interview
- Note **enthusiasm or hesitation** around certain topics
- Watch for **contradictions** between what they say and earlier survey responses
- Pay attention to **interaction types they spontaneously mention**
- Notice **whether they use parameter language** (even if unaware condition)
- Track **emotional responses** - frustration, excitement, confusion

### Post-Interview Documentation
- Immediately after: Write reflection on key themes
- Note any **follow-up questions** needed for clarity
- Identify **quotes** that strongly illustrate RQ-related findings
- Flag any **anomalies** or unexpected responses
- Consider how this participant's experience **compares to others**

---

## ADAPTATION NOTES

### For Different Participant Types

**High-engagement participants (created multiple poems):**
- Ask them to compare experiences across different poems
- Probe deeper into evolution of their strategies
- Ask about learning trajectory across the session

**Low-engagement participants (minimal interaction):**
- Focus more on barriers and challenges
- Ask what would have made them engage more
- Explore whether it was technical, motivational, or conceptual issues

**Aware vs. Unaware conditions:**
- Tailor Q11-12 based on awareness condition
- For unaware participants, at end you can reveal parameters and ask if it changes their interpretation

**Different rooms (A, B, C, D):**
- Note parameter settings in your questions
- If they experienced very different chatbot behaviors, explore this explicitly
- Compare their experience to what you observed in chat logs

---

## ETHICAL REMINDERS

- **Confidentiality:** Remind participants their responses are anonymous
- **Voluntary:** Emphasize they can skip questions or stop anytime
- **No right answers:** Make clear you want honest opinions, not "correct" responses
- **Recording consent:** Confirm permission to record audio for transcription
- **Member checking:** Offer to share summary of findings for their feedback
