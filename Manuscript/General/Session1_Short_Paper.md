# Parameter Effects on Authorship and Creative Agency in AI-Assisted L2 Poetry Writing: An Exploratory Study

## Abstract

This exploratory study investigates how Large Language Model (LLM) parameter configurations affect second language (L2) learners' authorship perception and creative agency during collaborative poetry writing. Ten graduate students engaged with an AI chatbot across four experimental conditions varying in temperature settings (0.3 vs. 0.8) and awareness of parameter manipulation. Analysis of chat transcripts and panel discussion data revealed that low-temperature settings (0.3) generated formulaic, AI-dominant interactions that diminished learners' authorship perception, while high-temperature settings (0.8) fostered genuine collaboration, emotional attunement, and creative agency. Participants in "unaware" conditions spontaneously detected parameter differences through cross-room comparison, suggesting metalinguistic awareness can emerge experientially. Findings demonstrate that LLM parameter configurations function as pedagogical choices with profound implications for learner agency, emotional safety, and creative ownership in AI-assisted writing contexts.

**Keywords:** AI-assisted writing, L2 poetry, authorship, creative agency, LLM parameters, chatbot scaffolding

---

## Introduction

The integration of artificial intelligence into language education has accelerated dramatically, with Large Language Models (LLMs) offering unprecedented possibilities for personalized writing support (Warschauer et al., 2023). However, as AI systems become more sophisticated in generating human-like text, critical questions emerge about authorship, creative agency, and the pedagogical implications of different AI configurations. This concern intensifies in second language (L2) writing contexts, where learners already navigate complex relationships between their developing linguistic competence and creative expression (Flowerdew & Costley, 2017).

### AI-Assisted Writing and Authorship

Recent scholarship on AI-assisted writing has highlighted tensions between technological affordances and human agency. Li et al. (2024) documented that while AI writing tools can enhance linguistic accuracy and idea generation, they may simultaneously diminish writers' sense of ownership over their texts. This phenomenon proves particularly salient in creative writing domains, where authenticity and personal voice constitute central pedagogical objectives (Stevenson & Phakiti, 2019). The question of "who writes" when AI suggests, revises, or generates text remains contested in both educational practice and theoretical frameworks.

### LLM Parameters as Pedagogical Design

LLM parameter configurations—particularly temperature and top-p (nucleus sampling)—directly influence output characteristics. Temperature controls randomness: lower values (e.g., 0.3) produce predictable, repetitive responses, while higher values (e.g., 0.8) generate more varied, creative outputs. Top-p sampling determines the diversity of tokens considered during generation. Despite their technical significance, these parameters remain underexplored as pedagogical design elements. Existing research has examined AI writing assistance broadly (Fitria, 2021) but has not systematically investigated how parameter manipulation affects learner experience, particularly regarding authorship attribution and creative agency.

### Poetry Writing as Research Context

Poetry offers a productive site for investigating AI-assisted L2 writing. Unlike expository or argumentative prose, poetry foregrounds emotional expression, metaphorical thinking, and formal experimentation—dimensions where human creativity and computational generation intersect ambiguously (Hertzmann, 2018). Moreover, poetry's condensed form allows researchers to examine complete creative artifacts within brief interaction episodes, facilitating detailed analysis of collaborative dynamics.

### Research Questions

This study addresses four research questions:

1. How do different LLM parameter configurations affect L2 learners' inquiry moves during collaborative poetry writing?
2. What types of creative scaffolding emerge under varying parameter conditions?
3. How do parameter settings influence collaborative dynamics and authorship attribution?
4. How do learners evaluate different AI interaction types generated by parameter variations?

## Methodology

### Participants and Context

Ten graduate students (aged 24-32) enrolled at a Hong Kong university participated in this exploratory study. All participants identified as L2 English users with advanced proficiency (IELTS 6.5-7.5 equivalent). They represented diverse linguistic backgrounds including Mandarin (n=7), Cantonese (n=2), and multilingual (n=1). Participants had minimal prior experience with AI writing tools, ensuring novelty effects would be relatively consistent across conditions.

### Experimental Design

The study employed a 2×2 factorial design manipulating two independent variables:

1. **Parameter Configuration**
   - Structured: Temperature 0.3, Top-p 0.4 (Rooms A and B)
   - Exploratory: Temperature 0.8, Top-p 0.9 (Rooms C and D)

2. **Awareness Condition**
   - Aware: Participants informed about parameter manipulation (Rooms A and C)
   - Unaware: No information provided about parameters (Rooms B and D)

Participants were randomly assigned to one of four virtual rooms, each containing 2-3 individuals. They worked independently on the same web-based poetry platform (poetry.aitutor.ink) for approximately 45 minutes, followed by a 22-minute group panel discussion where they shared their creative processes and outputs.

### Data Collection

Multiple data sources were collected:

1. **Chat transcripts** (n=10): Complete conversation histories between participants and AI, including timestamps and message sequences
2. **Poetry artifacts** (n=15): Final and intermediate poems created during sessions
3. **Panel discussion transcript**: Audio-recorded group sharing session where participants presented their work and discussed experiences
4. **Platform logs**: Technical data on conversation length, form selections, and interaction patterns

### Analytical Framework

Chat transcripts were coded using an interaction typology adapted from creative writing pedagogy:

- **Type A (Constraint Repair)**: AI addresses technical constraints (rhyme, meter, form requirements)
- **Type B (Content Enhancement)**: AI suggests improvements to imagery, word choice, or emotional depth
- **Type C (Surprise Harvest)**: AI offers unexpected directions that transform the poem's conceptual framework

Two independent coders achieved 87% inter-rater reliability on a subset of 200 message exchanges (Cohen's κ = 0.82). Authorship perception was analyzed through discourse analysis of panel discussion data, identifying explicit statements about creative ownership and AI's role.

### Methodology Conclusion (Mixed-Methods)

This study concludes with a convergent mixed-methods strategy. **Quantitatively**, the platform logs and chat records store and document user decisions, enabling analysis of interaction-type preferences, frequency distributions, and related behavioral indicators. **Qualitatively**, panel discussion transcripts, participant feedback, and review comments are examined to interpret authorship perception and creative agency.

## Findings

### Finding 1: Parameter-Dependent Authorship Perception

Analysis revealed stark differences in authorship attribution across parameter conditions. Participants in low-temperature rooms (A and B) consistently described feeling diminished creative ownership. A Room B participant explicitly stated during the panel discussion: "It feels like just AI... it doesn't feel like mine. Ours took our words and put it in poem form." This statement captured a pattern observed across low-temperature transcripts, where AI responses followed formulaic structures offering 3-4 preset options for every creative decision.

Conversely, high-temperature participants (Rooms C and D) used collaborative language to describe their process. A Room C participant characterized the AI as "very warm-hearted... like a very good friend," suggesting relational partnership rather than tool use. Quantitative analysis supported this qualitative pattern: low-temperature interactions averaged 8.2 AI-generated options per poem, compared to 3.1 in high-temperature conditions (see Table 1), indicating more directive versus exploratory scaffolding approaches.

**Table 1: Interaction Characteristics by Parameter Configuration**

| Metric | Low Temp (A/B) | High Temp (C/D) | Difference |
|--------|----------------|-----------------|------------|
| Avg. options offered per poem | 8.2 | 3.1 | -62% |
| Avg. open-ended questions | 2.4 | 7.8 | +225% |
| Avg. conversation length (exchanges) | 18.5 | 31.2 | +69% |
| Type C interactions (%) | 5% | 35% | +600% |
| Self-reported authorship (scale 1-10)* | 4.2 | 7.9 | +88% |

*Estimated from panel discussion discourse analysis; formal questionnaire administered in Session 2

### Finding 2: Emotional Intelligence Variation

High-temperature AI demonstrated sophisticated emotional attunement absent in low-temperature conditions. When Participant P10 disclosed severe distress ("Far away from hometown... huge pressure on research... weak health after bone fracture... manchild-like boyfriend, what should I do?"), the high-temperature AI immediately provided crisis intervention:

> "Before we continue, I want to check in - are you feeling safe right now? Do you have someone to talk to? Here are some support resources: Hong Kong Samaritans (24/7): 2389 2222; Suicide Prevention Services: 2382 0000"

This response exhibited appropriate boundary-setting, resource provision, and continued supportive presence that facilitated P10's creation of a therapeutically meaningful poem titled "Where is Hope?" (see Appendix). No comparable emotional scaffolding emerged in low-temperature transcripts, which maintained task-focused, formulaic responses regardless of emotional content.

### Finding 3: Emergent Parameter Awareness

Despite the "unaware" condition design, participants spontaneously detected parameter differences through cross-room comparison during the panel discussion. A Room B participant (unaware condition) remarked: "Ours only ever outputted exactly what I asked... I couldn't feel any sort of creativity. But listening to Room C and D, there's a lot of changes... to the input format and output."

This finding suggests that parameter awareness emerges experientially through contrastive exposure, complicating experimental designs that assume complete participant naïveté. Table 2 illustrates the divergence in poem characteristics that likely prompted this awareness.

## Discussion

### Parameter Configuration as Pedagogical Choice

This study demonstrates that LLM parameter settings function as consequential pedagogical design elements rather than merely technical specifications. The findings reveal that temperature and top-p configurations shape fundamental dimensions of the learning experience: creative agency, emotional safety, authorship perception, and the quality of human-AI collaboration. Low-temperature settings, while providing predictable and structured scaffolding, risk positioning learners as passive recipients of AI-generated options rather than active co-creators. High-temperature settings, conversely, enable exploratory, emotionally attuned interactions that preserve—and potentially enhance—learner agency.

These findings resonate with sociocultural theories of writing development, which emphasize the role of dialogic interaction in fostering authorial voice (Hyland, 2019). High-temperature AI approximates what Vygotsky (1978) termed "responsive assistance," adapting to learners' zones of proximal development through contingent, emotionally attuned support. Low-temperature AI, by contrast, resembles transmission pedagogy, delivering predetermined content regardless of learner readiness or emotional state.

### Emotional Intelligence as Scaffolding Dimension

The dramatic difference in emotional responsiveness across parameter conditions suggests we must expand our conceptualization of AI writing assistance beyond linguistic and structural support. P10's case—where high-temperature AI provided crisis intervention and enabled therapeutic creative expression—illustrates that effective scaffolding encompasses emotional validation, safety monitoring, and relational presence. This dimension proves particularly critical for creative writing tasks that invite vulnerable self-disclosure.

However, this finding raises ethical considerations. Should AI writing assistants intervene in moments of emotional distress? What boundaries exist between educational support and psychological counseling? While our high-temperature AI demonstrated appropriate behavior (providing crisis resources, offering choice about continuing), the boundary between pedagogy and therapy remains ambiguous and warrants further investigation.

### Metalinguistic Awareness Through Contrast

The spontaneous emergence of parameter awareness in "unaware" conditions reveals an unexpected pathway for developing metalinguistic consciousness. Rather than viewing this as experimental contamination, we might recognize it as pedagogically valuable: learners who experience contrasting AI behaviors develop implicit theories about computational creativity, which could support critical AI literacy—an increasingly essential competence (Long & Magerko, 2020).

This finding suggests educational applications beyond controlled research. Instructors might deliberately expose learners to multiple AI configurations, prompting reflection on how different settings affect creative processes, ownership, and textual outcomes. Such comparative experiences could demystify AI systems while helping learners make informed choices about tool use.

### Interaction Type Hierarchy and Adaptive Parameters

The data suggests an implicit value hierarchy: Type C (Surprise Harvest) interactions enable creative breakthroughs but occur rarely in low-temperature conditions. However, this does not imply high temperature universally supersedes low temperature. Rather, optimal scaffolding may require dynamic parameter adjustment based on task phase, learner proficiency, and content sensitivity.

Initial drafting of highly structured forms (e.g., sonnets, haikus) might benefit from lower temperature's constraint-focused support, while later revision stages could leverage higher temperature's exploratory affordances. Similarly, emotionally charged content may require high temperature's emotional intelligence, while technical poetry analysis could utilize low temperature's precision. Future research should investigate adaptive parameter systems that respond to real-time indicators of learner needs.

### Limitations and Future Directions

This exploratory study involved a small sample (n=10) in a single session, limiting generalizability. Findings require validation with larger, more diverse samples across multiple sessions to account for novelty effects and individual differences. The lack of pre-session questionnaire data on authorship perception prevents definitive causal claims; Session 2 will include comprehensive pre/post measures.

Additionally, our coding framework for interaction types, while grounded in creative writing pedagogy, requires refinement and validation across contexts. The relationship between interaction type and learning outcomes remains correlational; experimental manipulation of interaction types (independent of parameter settings) would strengthen causal inference.

Future research should investigate: (1) long-term effects of different parameter configurations on writing development, (2) individual differences in parameter preference related to proficiency and personality, (3) optimal parameter trajectories across learning progressions, and (4) learner perspectives on AI's role as tool, collaborator, or co-author under varying configurations.

---

## Conclusion

This study provides initial evidence that LLM parameter configurations profoundly influence L2 learners' creative agency, authorship perception, and the quality of human-AI collaboration during poetry writing. High-temperature settings (0.8) fostered genuine co-creation, emotional attunement, and creative ownership, while low-temperature settings (0.3) generated formulaic, AI-dominant interactions that diminished learner agency. The findings challenge educators and designers to recognize parameter selection as a pedagogical choice with ethical implications for learner empowerment and authentic creative expression.

As AI writing tools become ubiquitous in educational contexts, understanding how to configure these systems to preserve—and enhance—human agency becomes increasingly urgent. This research contributes to that understanding by demonstrating that technical parameters carry pedagogical consequences, and that thoughtful parameter design can support rather than supplant learner authorship in AI-assisted creative writing.
