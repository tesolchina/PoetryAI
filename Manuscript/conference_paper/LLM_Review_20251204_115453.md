**OVERALL ASSESSMENT**

This paper presents a timely and highly relevant investigation into the pedagogical implications of LLM parameter configurations in L2 creative writing. The core idea—that technical parameters systematically shape human-AI interaction types with profound effects on learner authorship and satisfaction—is novel and impactful. The preliminary findings, particularly the "Type B paradox" and the predictive power of "Surprise Harvest" (Type C) for authorship, are compelling and hold significant pedagogical value. However, the current manuscript struggles with narrative flow in the introduction, prematurely introduces AI, and could benefit from a more explicit articulation of its research objectives and a deeper grounding of its theoretical framework in prior L2 and human-AI collaboration literature. Addressing these structural and content issues will significantly strengthen the paper's academic rigor and clarity.

**SPECIFIC COMMENTS ON SIMON'S CONCERNS**

**1. Introduce creative writing problems more deeply**

*   **Assessment of current state:** The paper touches upon challenges in L2 creative writing (linguistic competence vs. creative expression, limited exemplars, individualized feedback, authorship/creative agency) in Section 1.1. However, it quickly pivots to AI as a solution without fully elaborating on the *extent* and *impact* of these problems. The introduction of specific pedagogical challenges feels somewhat perfunctory rather than deeply established. For instance, while "limited access to diverse exemplars" is mentioned, the *consequences* of this limitation (e.g., formulaic writing, lack of originality, cultural bias) are not fully explored. The "questions of authorship and creative agency" are introduced as "urgent as AI writing assistants enter L2 classrooms," implying AI *creates* these problems, rather than AI offering a *potential solution* to pre-existing, deeply rooted pedagogical dilemmas in creative writing.

*   **Specific suggestions for improvement:** Dedicate more space to painting a vivid picture of the difficulties L2 learners and instructors face in creative writing. Emphasize the *pedagogical gaps* that AI *could* potentially fill, rather than immediately presenting AI as the answer.

*   **Concrete examples/recommendations:**
    *   **Expand on "limited access to diverse exemplars":** Discuss how this leads to monocultural perspectives, formulaic writing, or a lack of individual voice. Mention how L2 learners often struggle to break free from grammatical constraints to embrace creative risks.
    *   **Elaborate on "insufficient individualized feedback":** Explain why this is particularly acute in creative writing—it's subjective, time-consuming, and requires deep linguistic and cultural understanding, which is often beyond the capacity of a single L2 instructor for a large class. Connect this to the high cognitive load for L2 writers.
    *   **Deepen the "authorship and creative agency" problem:** Before AI, how did traditional methods sometimes stifle agency? E.g., over-reliance on imitation, fear of error, difficulty expressing nuanced emotions in an L2. Frame AI as a *new lens* through which to examine these *existing* challenges, rather than the *cause* of the urgency.
    *   **Consider adding a paragraph or two** in Section 1.1 that specifically details the *consequences* of these challenges for L2 learners' motivation, engagement, and ultimately, their creative output. For example, "These pedagogical limitations often result in L2 learners producing texts that are grammatically correct but lack personal voice, exhibit limited stylistic range, and fail to evoke genuine creative satisfaction, leading to diminished motivation for creative expression in the L2."

**2. Postpone AI assistance introduction**

*   **Assessment of current state:** AI is introduced almost immediately in the abstract and Section 1.1 ("challenges AI assistance might address," "urgent as AI writing assistants enter L2 classrooms"). Section 1.2 then directly discusses "AI as Collaborative Partner." This jump is too quick and doesn't allow the reader to fully grasp the *human* problems before the *AI* solution is presented.

*   **Specific suggestions for improvement:** Restructure the introduction to first establish the deep-seated pedagogical problems in L2 creative writing, then introduce AI as a *potential avenue* for addressing these problems, acknowledging both its promise and its complexities (e.g., ethical concerns, black box nature, potential for over-reliance) before diving into its specific affordances.

*   **Concrete examples/recommendations:**
    *   **Revised Introduction Flow (see Structural Revision Suggestions below):** Start with a robust discussion of L2 creative writing pedagogy and its inherent difficulties. This could be Section 1.1.
    *   **Section 1.2 could then introduce AI more cautiously:** "In recent years, the emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) has prompted discussions about their potential to transform various domains, including education. While the integration of AI into pedagogical practices raises significant questions, its capacity to generate varied text and interact conversationally offers a novel lens through which to re-examine long-standing challenges in L2 creative writing instruction. This paper explores how..."
    *   **Explicitly state the *gap* AI might fill:** "Given the aforementioned difficulties in providing individualized feedback and diverse exemplars, LLMs present a unique opportunity to scaffold creative processes in ways previously unimaginable, potentially fostering greater autonomy and creative exploration for L2 learners."

**3. Clarify research objectives/questions**

*   **Assessment of current state:** The "Research Focus and Contributions" (Section 1.5) provides a good overview of the paper's aims, but it reads more like a summary of contributions than explicit research questions. The abstract states the study "examines how LLM parameter configurations affect authorship perception and creative satisfaction in AI-assisted L2 poetry writing," which is a good start. However, the connection between "parameter configurations," "interaction types," and the "outcomes" (authorship, satisfaction, motivation) could be more directly articulated as clear research questions.

*   **Specific suggestions for improvement:** Formulate 2-3 clear, concise research questions (RQs) that directly guide the study and address the stated aims. These RQs should explicitly link the independent variables (parameter configurations) to the mediating variables (interaction types) and the dependent variables (authorship, satisfaction, motivation/agency).

*   **Concrete examples/recommendations:**
    *   **Consider placing RQs at the end of the Introduction (e.g., Section 1.6 or within 1.5).**
    *   **Possible RQs:**
        *   RQ1: How do different LLM parameter configurations (e.g., low vs. high temperature) systematically affect the distribution of human-AI interaction types (Constraint Repair, Exemplar Giving, Surprise Harvest) in L2 creative writing?
        *   RQ2: How do these distinct human-AI interaction types, particularly Surprise Harvest, influence L2 learners' perceptions of authorship and creative satisfaction in AI-assisted poetry writing?
        *   RQ3: What are the pedagogical implications of LLM parameter choices for fostering L2 creative agency and motivation in AI-assisted writing environments?
    *   Ensure the abstract and conclusion directly address these RQs.

**4. Expand discussion of three engagement types with prior studies**

*   **Assessment of current state:** Section 1.4, "Theoretical Framework: Three Interaction Types," introduces Constraint Repair, Exemplar Giving, and Surprise Harvest. While it cites a few relevant papers (Hanauer, Lyster & Ranta, Coenen et al.), the discussion for each type is relatively brief. There's an opportunity to more deeply ground each interaction type in existing literature from L2 writing, human-computer interaction (HCI), and AI-assisted creativity.

*   **Specific suggestions for improvement:** For each interaction type, provide a more substantial discussion that:
    *   Clearly defines the type.
    *   Connects it explicitly to relevant theories or pedagogical concepts in L2 learning (e.g., feedback, scaffolding, zone of proximal development, genre pedagogy).
    *   References specific prior studies that exemplify or discuss similar interaction patterns, even if not explicitly labeled with the same terminology.
    *   Explains *why* this type is particularly relevant or challenging for L2 creative writers.

*   **Concrete examples/recommendations:**
    *   **Constraint Repair:**
        *   Beyond Lyster & Ranta (1997), consider literature on *written corrective feedback* in L2 writing (e.g., Ferris, 2004; Bitchener & Ferris, 2012). Discuss the benefits (accuracy, form-focused attention) and potential drawbacks (over-correction, stifling creativity, focus on form over meaning) when AI provides such feedback. How does AI's *mode* of delivery (e.g., direct correction vs. suggestion) compare to human feedback?
        *   "This aligns with research on written corrective feedback in L2 writing, where focused attention on linguistic errors can improve accuracy (Ferris, 2004). However, traditional WCF often struggles with individualization and can sometimes be perceived as stifling creativity if not balanced with attention to content (Bitchener & Ferris, 2012). AI's capacity for immediate, context-specific error identification..."
    *   **Exemplar Giving:**
        *   Deepen the connection to Hanauer (2010) and *imitation-transformation*. Discuss *genre pedagogy* or *text-modeling* in L2 writing (e.g., Hyland, 2007; Tardy, 2006). How do L2 writers typically use models? What are the benefits (reducing cognitive load, learning genre conventions) and risks (plagiarism, lack of originality, over-reliance)?
        *   "Beyond general scaffolding, Exemplar Giving draws heavily from principles of genre pedagogy (Hyland, 2007) and text-modeling in L2 writing (Tardy, 2006), where learners analyze and appropriate features from model texts. For L2 writers, access to diverse and contextually appropriate exemplars can significantly reduce cognitive load and facilitate the acquisition of stylistic conventions. However, a pedagogical challenge lies in moving beyond mere imitation to genuine transformation and individual voice (Hanauer, 2010)."
    *   **Surprise Harvest:**
        *   Beyond Coenen et al. (2022) and "serendipitous discovery," explore concepts like *divergent thinking* (Guilford, 1967), *creative friction* in human-AI collaboration (e.g., Shneiderman, 2007 on "creative design space"), or the role of *novelty* in creativity (Boden, 2004). How does unexpected input challenge L2 writers? Does it push them beyond their linguistic comfort zone or open new expressive avenues?
        *   "This concept resonates with theories of divergent thinking (Guilford, 1967) and the role of novelty in fostering creativity (Boden, 2004). In human-AI collaboration, 'serendipitous discovery' (Coenen et al., 2022) is often considered crucial for expanding creative search spaces beyond human preconceptions. For L2 writers, such unexpected input could be particularly transformative, potentially helping them overcome lexical or structural constraints that might otherwise limit their creative expression, pushing them into a 'zone of proximal development' for creative language use."
