# Discussion: Parameter Configuration as DDL's Pedagogical Pivot

## Opening: Resolving Crosthwaite's Challenge

Crosthwaite (2023) questioned whether generative AI represents "the death of DDL" by allowing learners to bypass inductive pattern discovery. Our findings reveal a more nuanced reality: **AI represents neither DDL's death nor simple continuation, but a pedagogical watershed where parameter configuration determines which path unfolds**. Temperature and top-p settings function not as technical specifications but as pedagogical design variables fundamentally reshaping learning transactions between human creativity and computational capability.

This discussion interprets Session 1 findings through three lenses: (1) how parameter manipulation operationalizes dynamic DDL scaffolding, (2) why the "helpful but alienating" paradox reveals tensions in AI-mediated creative pedagogy, and (3) what these mechanisms suggest for designing AI tools preserving learner agency while providing transformative support.

---

## 1. The Parameter→Interaction→Perception Mechanism

### 1.1 The Seven-Fold Difference: Parameter as Pedagogical Lever

Our most striking finding�?*the seven-fold increase in Type C (Surprise Harvest) interactions from structured to exploratory parameters** (5% to 35%, *p* < .001, Cramér's V = .38)—demonstrates parameter configuration as a **pedagogical lever** with immediate, measurable effects. This is not subtle influence but fundamental transformation. When parameters shift from low (temperature 0.3, top-p 0.4) to high (0.8, 0.9), AI's role metamorphoses from **pattern-enforcer** to **pattern-extender**, from **curator of corpus-typical options** to **generator of corpus-divergent provocations**.

In structured rooms, learners encountered AI as **meticulous editor**—catching deviations, offering polished alternatives, providing linguistic safety nets. The experience felt orderly and predictable: "It took our words and tried to put it in poem form." In exploratory rooms, learners encountered **imaginative interlocutor** suggesting unexpected techniques ("found poetry" using legal citations), posing philosophical questions ("Do you want the poem to condemn or understand?"), and venturing creative risks: "a very warm-hearted AI... like a very good friend, not just a machine."

This seven-fold difference operationalizes what traditional DDL could never achieve: **adaptive pattern engagement intensity**. Johns' (1991) DDL vision positioned learners as active "research workers" discovering patterns through corpus interrogation with uniform engagement intensity. AI enables **system-initiated engagement intensity calibration**—parameter settings determine whether learners encounter predominantly corpus-typical patterns or corpus-extending provocations. This represents **DDL evolution**, not death, shifting from learner-initiated corpus searches to **selective uptake of system-initiated provocations** while preserving DDL's core insight: **patterns discovered are better learned than rules transmitted**.

### 1.2 The "Helpful but Alienating" Paradox

Finding 2.1 revealed disturbing tension: **75% rated Type B (Exemplar Giving) interactions as "most helpful," yet Type B frequency negatively correlated with authorship perception** (*r* = -.58, *p* < .05). This paradox exposes contradictions in conceptualizing pedagogical support in creative domains.

Type B provides immediate assistance—presenting concrete options addressing linguistic gaps, reducing decision paralysis, enabling task completion. For L2 learners, this support feels genuinely helpful: "AI help me to generate few sentences let me choose." Yet this same participant reported only 20% self-authorship: "I just need to choose use what sentence wrote by AI. I only own the meaning on this poem." Type B positions learners as **selectors rather than creators**, shifting cognitive labor from generation to evaluation. Learners contribute **intent and judgment** but not **linguistic instantiation**—owning "meaning" but not "making."

This contradicts DDL principles. Boulton (2012) emphasized DDL's pedagogical power stems from "hands-on" engagement—learners actively interrogate corpora and internalize discoveries through sustained interaction. Type B **reverses this dynamic**: instead of learners constructing understanding from patterns, AI constructs options and learners choose. The "hands-on" engagement becomes "hands-off" selection, short-circuiting the developmental process building creative competence.

Our findings suggest **parameter configuration directly impacts this scaffolding-autonomy balance**. Structured parameters produce predominantly Type B interactions, maximizing accessibility but risking authorship alienation. Exploratory parameters shift toward Type C interactions preserving agency through **provocative suggestion rather than predetermined selection**—offering **possibilities to consider** rather than **options to choose**.

### 1.3 Type C as DDL-Compatible Collaboration

Finding 2.2 demonstrated the sole participant preferring Type C reported **80% self-authorship**—the highest despite extensive AI collaboration. Chat analysis revealed sustained conceptual dialogue rather than preset options: AI posed philosophical questions, suggested unexpected techniques, offered structural feedback while honoring creative vision. Critically, the participant **rejected, modified, and synthesized** suggestions selectively—exercising authorial control throughout.

The authorship rationale illuminates why this felt collaborative: "The general theme, the idea to use a turnstile as the central metaphor... these are all my ideas. AI helped me think about whether I want to write something that indicts or something that empathizes." **Conceptual ownership remained with the learner** (theme, metaphor, stance), while AI provided **reflective provocation and craft guidance**. The participant generated language; AI provoked thinking about that language.

Type C interactions enable mutuality: AI generates **unexpected possibilities** (pattern extension beyond corpus frequencies), and learners generate **selective uptake and linguistic instantiation** (transforming provocations into personal expression). Both exercise creativity complementarily rather than competitively.

Traditional DDL operates within **corpus-bounded possibility space**—discovering patterns that exist in data. Type C, enabled through exploratory parameters, **extends beyond corpus frequencies into generative possibility space**. AI becomes capable of suggesting connections, techniques, and framings emerging through novel pattern recombination rather than corpus retrieval. This transforms AI from **pattern-revealer** to **pattern-extender**, preserving DDL's constructivist foundation while expanding creative potential.

## 2. Answering Crosthwaite: Conditional Resolution

Crosthwaite (2023) asked whether AI would "do the work for learners," bypassing DDL's inductive engagement. Our findings provide **conditional answer**: AI undermines or supports DDL principles **depending on parameter configuration**.

**Under structured parameters**, AI functions as **DDL antithesis**—generating corpus-typical exemplars positioning learners as selectors rather than discoverers. Pattern exposure becomes passive reception; task completion occurs at authorship's expense. This validates Crosthwaite's concern.

**Under exploratory parameters**, AI functions as **DDL evolution**—generating corpus-extending provocations positioning learners as co-constructors. Pattern engagement becomes active synthesis; learners exercise selective uptake, critical judgment, and linguistic instantiation—capacities central to both DDL and creative development.

**The crucial recognition**: Parameter settings are not technical specifications but **pedagogical design variables** determining whether AI constrains or enables, scaffolds appropriately or over-determines outcomes. This insight transforms responsible AI integration from whether to use AI (presence is inevitable) to **how to configure AI** serving learning goals.

---

## 3. Pedagogical Implications

### 3.1 Parameter Literacy as Essential Competence

Our findings establish **parameter literacy** as essential professional competence for educators integrating AI into L2 writing. This includes understanding how temperature and top-p shape AI behavior, exercising pedagogical judgment about when to use structured versus exploratory parameters, and identifying tools exposing parameters for educator control.

### 3.2 Design Recommendations

Educational AI tools should:
- **Make parameters visible and adjustable** with pedagogical guidance
- **Provide preset configurations** labeled by function ("Form Practice," "Creative Exploration")
- **Display interaction analytics** showing Type A/B/C distributions for metacognitive reflection
- **Support parameter experimentation** allowing learners to compare configurations

This transforms AI from passive response generator to **configurable pedagogical scaffold** whose affordances can be deliberately shaped.

### 3.3 Curricular Sequencing

Findings suggest **progressive parameter curriculum**: structured parameters (weeks 1-3) building foundations, moderate parameters (weeks 4-6) developing voice, exploratory parameters (weeks 7-9) enabling creative independence. This **systematic fading through parameter adjustment** operationalizes scaffolding principles without constant instructor monitoring.

---

## 4. Limitations

Findings represent **preliminary evidence with N=10** requiring full dataset validation (target N=20). The study examined **L2 poetry writing exclusively**—parameter effects might differ across academic writing, professional communication, or narrative fiction. Our 2×2 design compared **fixed parameter configurations** without examining progressive adjustment or participant-controlled parameters. We measured **immediate authorship perception** during 45-minute sessions—long-term developmental outcomes remain unknown. Future research should address these limitations through longitudinal studies, cross-task investigations, and comparisons with traditional instruction.

---

## 5. Conclusion: The Hinge of DDL's Future

AI represents neither DDL's death nor simple continuation, but a **pedagogical hinge where parameter configuration determines which future unfolds**. With structured parameters, AI validates Crosthwaite's concern—creating "helpful but alienating" experiences where task completion occurs at authorship's expense. With exploratory parameters, AI enables DDL evolution—preserving creative agency while providing transformative support through corpus-extending provocations.

The Parameter→Interaction→Perception pathway establishes **parameter literacy as essential competence** for educators navigating AI integration. Temperature and top-p settings are not technical minutiae but pedagogical design variables determining learning outcomes. DDL's future depends not on AI's capabilities but **our capacity to exercise pedagogical judgment over its configuration**.

The death Crosthwaite feared would come not from AI's existence but from failing to understand and control how it shapes learning. The evolution we demonstrate becomes possible when educators recognize **parameters as pedagogical levers** and design accordingly. Our most consequential contribution: demonstrating **generative AI is not a pedagogical intervention but a configurable platform** whose affordances must be deliberately shaped through informed design.

Parameter configuration is **pedagogical decision-making disguised as engineering specification**. Making it visible, adjustable, and pedagogically grounded transforms AI from potential threat into powerful partner in DDL's ongoing evolution. The difference between alienation and authorship, between constraint and creativity, between DDL's death and evolution—may hinge on adjusting a temperature setting from 0.3 to 0.8.
